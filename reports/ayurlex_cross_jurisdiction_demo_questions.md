# AYURLEX Cross-Jurisdiction Comparative Analysis
**Document ID:** REP-CROSS-JUR-V2

## 1. The Cross-Border IP Challenge in Botanical Medicine

Ayurvedic and herbal companies face diametrically opposed patent and regulatory standards across key global markets:
- In **India**, Section 3(p) explicitly excludes traditional knowledge and Section 3(d) excludes new uses of known substances.
- In the **United States**, 35 U.S.C. 101 excludes natural products under the Alice/Mayo doctrine, but allows novel extraction processes and synergistic synthetic compositions.
- In **Europe**, EPC Article 54(5) explicitly allows second medical use claims for known botanical substances if a new therapeutic application is demonstrated.
- In **Food/Dietary Regulations**, India's FSSAI Ayurveda Aahara (2022) differs fundamentally from US FDA DSHEA (1994) and EU THMPD (2004/24/EC).

## 2. Comparative Demo Questions

### Comparison 1: India (Section 3e) vs United States (35 U.S.C. 101/103)
- **ID:** `Q-COMP-IN-US`
- **Query:** *"Compare the patent eligibility of synergistic herbal extracts between India Section 3(e) and US 35 U.S.C. 101/103 Alice-Mayo framework."*
- **Key Legal Insight Surfaced:**
  - India requires demonstrable *super-additive efficacy* to overcome the Section 3(e) mere admixture bar.
  - US requires demonstrating that the formulation has *markedly different characteristics* or an *inventive concept* beyond a product of nature under Mayo/Myriad.

### Comparison 2: India (Section 3p/3d) vs European Patent Office (EPC Art 54(5))
- **ID:** `Q-COMP-IN-EP`
- **Query:** *"What are the differences between Indian Section 3(p) exclusion and European EPC Article 54(5) second medical use for Ayurvedic botanicals?"*
- **Key Legal Insight Surfaced:**
  - Indian law strictly prohibits patenting second medical uses of known substances under Section 3(d) and traditional knowledge under Section 3(p).
  - EPO allows purpose-limited product claims (*"Substance X for use in treating Disease Y"*) under EPC Article 54(5), creating a major patenting opportunity in Europe that is unavailable in India.

### Comparison 3: India FSSAI vs European Union THMPD Directive 2004/24/EC
- **ID:** `Q-COMP-IN-EU-REG`
- **Query:** *"What is the export pathway for classical Ayurvedic formulations from India into the European Union under Directive 2004/24/EC (THMPD)?"*
- **Key Legal Insight Surfaced:**
  - EU THMPD requires proof of traditional medicinal use for at least 30 years, including at least 15 years within the European Community. Classical Indian AFI citations satisfy the 30-year requirement, but exporters must provide 15 years of European bibliographic evidence.
